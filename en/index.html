21 October 2026Nature

Mapping the insula: how the brain “feels” the body

Neuroscientists studied the insula, a region deep in the brain, in 87 patients who had electrodes implanted for medical reasons. Weak electrical pulses made people feel heat, tingling, numbness and even nausea. The insula turned out to have three zones: the back handles sensations in specific body parts, the middle handles signals from internal organs, and the front links them to emotion and thinking.

21 October 2026Nature

What the Denisovans looked like: new skulls from northern China

Denisovans are mysterious ancient human relatives known for a long time mostly from DNA. Chinese researchers studied skull fragments, teeth and jaws from the Xujiayao site in Hebei province, roughly 140,000–200,000 years old. Ancient proteins and bone shape point to Denisovans, giving the group a recognisable “face” for the first time.

21 October 2026Nature

Plastic that breaks down even on the ocean floor

Compostable plastics degrade poorly at sea, where the right microbes are scarce. Researchers embedded enzymes into four kinds of polyester plastic so that it breaks down from within. In coastal water a PBSA film with just 0.1% enzyme vanished completely in a month, and at a depth of 757 metres samples almost fully degraded within five months.
